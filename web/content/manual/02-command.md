# Volume 2: Command

This volume sets out who commands whom in the UEE 9th Fleet, how posts are filled, and how orders are given. It builds on the posts defined in Volume 1.

## Principles of command

Six rules govern command in the fleet. Every other section applies them.

1. **One commander.** Every unit and every operation has exactly one commander and one named second-in-command.
2. **Authority follows the post.** A member commands because of the post they hold, not the rank they wear.
3. **On duty only.** Command authority exists during operations, training and staff duty. Off duty, nobody outranks anybody. The conduct rules apply at all times.
4. **Tell them what and why.** Orders state the task and its purpose and leave the method to the person doing it, inside the standing procedures.
5. **Correct before fast.** A commander delays an operation before stepping off unprepared.
6. **Values before methods.** Leaders may disagree with a method and change it through review. Nobody changes it in the middle of an operation.

## The chain of command

The home chain has five levels from the Fleet Commander to the team. Each commander has a senior enlisted adviser from the squadron upward.

| Level | Commander | Senior enlisted adviser | Primary posts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fleet | Fleet Commander | Fleet Master Chief | 1,429 |
| Battle group | Rear Admiral | Battle Group Master Chief | 564 |
| Squadron, battalion or Marine company | Commodore, Lieutenant Colonel or Captain | Squadron chief, Sergeant Major or Gunnery Sergeant | 124, 173 or 85 |
| Ship, flight, rifle company or platoon | Commanding officer, flight lead, or company or platoon commander | Chief of the Boat or platoon sergeant | 5 to 65 |
| Team | Team leader, section leader or skipper | None | 3 to 8 |

- **Span.** No commander directs more than about seven people. Above that, the deputy takes a share.
- **Senior enlisted.** They advise the commander and own the standards, training and welfare of the enlisted members. They are not a link in the chain for orders.
- **Skipping levels.** Orders and requests go one level at a time. Anyone may go straight to the conduct panel.
- **Staff.** Staff sections advise and act in their commander's name. They do not command units.

## Command on an operation

Every operation names its commander and second-in-command in the warning order. That pair commands everyone present, whatever rank they wear.

| Role | Who | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operation commander | Any qualified post-holder, named in the warning order | Commands everyone on the operation |
| Second-in-command | Named in the warning order | Takes over at once if the commander drops out |
| Net control | The control station of each net, callsign Zero. On the command net it is a signaller | Enforces voice procedure. On the command net, keeps the log |
| Observer | An instructor, on training and assessed operations | Watches and debriefs. Gives no orders. |

- **Rank on the night.** A senior member in a junior post follows the operation commander. This is how cadets command under supervision.
- **Task organisation.** Large operations group units into task groups, as Volume 1 describes. Units stay intact below that level.
- **Passing command.** If the commander disconnects, or is silent on the command net for two minutes, the second-in-command announces "I have command". Command returns only by a spoken handover.
- **After the second-in-command.** Command passes to the senior post-holder present.
- **Real-world problems.** Anyone may say "no duff" to suspend role-play for a real emergency.

## Succession and continuity

Every command post has a named deputy, and no key to the fleet is held by one person alone.

### Stepping up during an absence

When a commander is away, one person steps up and the ripple below is kept as short as possible.

| Absence | Who commands | Their own post | Rank | Recorded in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One operation | A stand-in for the night, normally the deputy | Kept. Gaps below are filled by stand-ins for that night. | No change | The attendance return |
| Up to 14 days | The deputy, covering | Kept. The next in line stands in on operations. | No change | Routine orders |
| 15 to 60 days | The deputy, as acting commander | Covered by an acting deputy | Acting rank for both | Routine orders, as appointments |
| More than 60 days | A new appointment | Released, or confirmed in the new post | Substantive | Routine orders |

- **Two levels at most.** A step-up creates an acting commander and, at most, an acting deputy. Everything below that is handled by stand-ins on the night, so the rest of the chain does not move.
- **Succession list.** Every unit publishes a list three deep. For the flagship it is the Executive Officer, the Tactical Officer and the Chief of the Boat.
- **Protected post.** Anyone who steps up goes back to their own post when the commander returns. Nobody loses a post by covering.
- **Up the chain.** The next commander up is told who is covering, and deals with that person as the commander.
- **Down the chain.** The unit is told at muster who has command. Orders from the stand-in carry the same weight.
- **Giving notice.** A member gives their commander the dates before an absence. A commander who misses two events without notice is covered as if absent.
- **Credit.** Nights in command go on the service record and count as observations for promotion.
- **Staff duties.** Each section head names a deputy from the pool, who covers in the same way.

### The Fleet Commander's deputy and the keys

The Fleet Commander's deputy changes as the fleet grows:

- Stages 2 to 6: the Executive Officer of the flagship.
- Stage 7: the Chief of Staff.
- Stage 8 onward: the Deputy Fleet Commander.

Two continuity rules protect the fleet itself:

- **Two key-holders.** From stage 3, two people hold each key: the RSI org, the domain, the hosting accounts, the Discord server, the voice service and the bank.
- **Named successor.** The Fleet Commander names a successor in writing. If none is named, the command board chooses one.

## Staff sections and the command board

Six staff sections do the fleet's administration. Each is led by a head and worked by a duty pool.

| Section | Led by | Does | Produces |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personnel and Recruiting | Head of Personnel and Recruiting | Applications, interviews, records, postings, activity and reserve | The strength return and routine orders |
| Training School | Commandant | Recruit training, the cadet course, qualifications and instructor standards | Qualified members and commissioned officers |
| Operations and Planning | Head of Operations and Planning | The event calendar, operation orders, the hostile list and the opposing force | Warning orders, operation orders and the lessons log |
| Logistics and Medical | Head of Logistics and Medical | The bank ledger, the asset register, ship readiness and the medical chain | The readiness return |
| Systems | Head of Systems | The platform, the voice app, accounts and access | A working platform and a net plan for each event |
| Conduct | A panel chair, on rotation | Complaints and discipline cases | Findings and recommendations |

- The Chief of Staff directs the sections from stage 7. Until then the Fleet Commander does.
- Each battle group mirrors the first four sections, with heads who hold the job as a secondary duty.

### The command board

The command board meets once a month and is where the fleet reviews itself. It advises, and the Fleet Commander decides.

Its members are the Fleet Commander, the deputy, the senior enlisted adviser and the section heads. Battle group commanders join from stage 7.

1. Strength against the stage thresholds, and whether a stage opens or freezes.
2. Recruiting and training numbers.
3. Lessons from operations and proposed changes to standing orders.
4. Appointments and promotions above unit level.
5. Conduct cases.
6. Money and assets.

Decisions are published in the next routine orders.

## Appointments

A post is filled by appointment from the level above, after the vacancy has been published to every eligible member.

1. The vacancy is published with its grade band and required qualifications.
2. Eligible members apply. Personnel checks grade, qualifications and activity.
3. The appointing commander selects, and the approving commander confirms.
4. The appointment is published in routine orders with its start date.

| Post | Appointed by | Approved by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry posts | Unit commander | Nobody else |
| Team leaders, skippers and chiefs | Unit commander | The next commander up |
| Ship commanding officers, flight leads, platoon and company commanders | Squadron or battalion commander | Battle group commander |
| Squadron, battalion and Marine company commanders | Battle group commander | Fleet Commander |
| Battle group commanders, fleet staff heads and senior fleet posts | Fleet Commander | The command board is consulted |
| Secondary duties | Section head | The member's own commander agrees |

- **Acting appointments** last up to 60 days. They are then confirmed, extended once or ended. An acting holder wears acting rank at the bottom of the post's band.
- **Promotion in post** is recommended by the unit commander and approved one level up.
- **Commissioning** is done only by the commissioning board after the cadet course. The board is the Commandant, one commander at O4 or above and one senior enlisted member.
- **Review.** Command appointments are reviewed every six months by whoever made them.
- **Removal.** The appointing commander may remove a holder who fails the standard or the activity minimum. Misconduct goes to the conduct panel.

## Orders

The fleet uses five kinds of order, from permanent standing orders down to a quick change in the middle of an operation.

| Order | Issued by | When | Contains |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standing orders | Fleet Commander | Permanent, changed by review | The doctrine volumes and the one-page cards |
| Routine orders | Personnel, for the commander | Weekly | Appointments, promotions, qualifications, the coming week and changes to standing orders |
| Warning order | Operation commander | At least 72 hours before | Task, time, the commander and the second-in-command |
| Operation order | Operation commander | At the brief, and in writing for larger operations | The five paragraphs below |
| Quick orders | Any commander | During an operation | What has changed, the new task and when |

### The operation order

| Paragraph | Contains |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Situation | Hostile forces and reports, friendly forces and the area |
| 2 Mission | One sentence: who, what, where, when, and in order to do what |
| 3 Execution | The commander's intent, the phases, a task for each unit, timings, the weapons state, and actions on contact, casualty and disconnect |
| 4 Support | Ships, fuel and rearming, the medical chain, the reinforcement point and the fallback ship |
| 5 Command and signal | The commander and second-in-command, succession, nets, callsigns and codewords |

A mission reads like this: "Task Force Jericho will patrol the lane between ArcCorp and microTech from 19:30 to 21:00, in order to deter piracy against traders."

- **One-third rule.** A commander uses no more than a third of the time available for their own planning and leaves the rest to the people below.
- **Back-brief.** Each subordinate leader briefs their plan back to the commander before step-off.
- **Written record.** Every order above a quick order is filed in the platform.

## Reports

Five reports go back up the chain, and all of them are filed in the platform.

| Report | From | When | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attendance return | Operation commander | At the end of each event | Activity records |
| After-action report | Operation commander | Within 48 hours | The lessons log and changes to standing orders |
| Strength return | Personnel | Monthly | The stage review at the command board |
| Readiness return | Logistics | Monthly | Which ships and crews can sail |
| Incident report | Anyone | When it happens | The conduct panel |

## Who decides what

Most decisions are made by whoever holds the post concerned. Eight are reserved.

| Decision | Decided by | After hearing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Declaring an org or a person hostile | Fleet Commander. From stage 7, battle group commanders in their own region. | Operations and Planning |
| Rules of engagement and standing orders | Fleet Commander | The command board |
| Opening or freezing a stage or a service | Fleet Commander | The command board, on the strength return |
| Alliances and joint operations with other orgs | Fleet Commander | The command board |
| Removing a member from the fleet | Fleet Commander, or a battle group commander for their own members | The conduct panel's finding |
| Spending from the bank | Head of Logistics up to 10 million aUEC a week, the Fleet Commander above it | Nobody else |
| Commissioning an officer | The commissioning board | The Training School |
| The weapons state on an operation | Operation commander | Nobody else |

## Command at launch

At launch the whole command structure is one person, so this volume applies in a cut-down form until stage 3.

| Stage | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The Fleet Commander is also commanding officer of the training ship, and names a second-in-command for each operation from whoever is attending. |
| 2 | The Fleet Commander commands UEES Nexus. An acting Executive Officer becomes the deputy. Weekly routine orders start. Instructing becomes the first secondary duty. |
| 3 | Section heads are appointed as secondary duties. The command board starts meeting. A second key-holder is named for every key. |
| 4 | Other members begin commanding patrols as operation commander. The first Army leader joins the board. |
| 5 | The Fleet Commander hands UEES Nexus to another commanding officer and commands the task force. |

Five things apply in full from the first operation:

- One commander and a named second-in-command.
- A warning order 72 hours out.
- An operation order in five paragraphs.
- An attendance return and an after-action report.
- "No duff" for real-world problems.
